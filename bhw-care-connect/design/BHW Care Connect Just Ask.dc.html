Where to go

ER or urgent care?

When you can’t wait for us and you’re not sure which door to walk through. When in doubt, choose the ER.

Emergency room · call 911

Go now, don’t drive yourself

Anything that could threaten life, limb, or eyesight.

  • Chest pain or pressure, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back
  • Face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech — note the time and call 911
  • Trouble breathing, or breathing that keeps getting worse
  • Bleeding that won’t stop with 10 minutes of firm pressure
  • A head injury with confusion, vomiting, or a blackout
  • Sudden severe abdominal pain, or vomiting blood
  • A seizure, or a fainting spell you can’t explain
  • Thoughts of harming yourself with a plan or the means to act
  • Severe allergic reaction — swelling of the lips or throat, hives with wheezing
  • An overdose or a poisoning, including a medication mix-up

Urgent care · walk in

Needs today, not an emergency

Send us a note afterward so it lands in your chart.

  • Fever, cough, sore throat, or an ear or sinus infection
  • Burning with urination or a suspected UTI
  • A cut that may need stitches but isn’t bleeding heavily
  • A sprain, a simple fracture, or a joint you can still move
  • Rashes, insect bites, or a minor burn
  • Vomiting or diarrhea when you can still keep sips down
  • Pink eye or a mild eye irritation
  • An asthma flare that improves with your inhaler
  • A tick bite, or a wound that needs cleaning
  • A work or school note you need the same day

Still unsure? Call 443.762.5343 during office hours, or press 1 after hours to reach the on-call clinician.

After a hospital or urgent care visit

Tell us you were seen

Records don’t always reach us, and when they do it can take a week. A two-line heads-up lets us pull your discharge paperwork, restart or stop the right medications, and get you a follow-up before anything unravels.

  • Best within 48 hours of going home — sooner if you were admitted.
  • Bring or photograph your discharge summary and any new prescriptions.
  • Most insurers cover a transition-of-care visit at no cost to you.
